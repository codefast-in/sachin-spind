For twenty years, getting found online meant one thing: ranking on Google. You wrote pages, earned links, and fought for a spot in the top ten blue links.

That is no longer the whole picture. People now ask Google a question and read the answer at the top of the page without clicking anything. They ask Siri or Alexa and hear one answer read aloud. They ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini or Claude, and get a written answer that mentions a handful of sources, or none.

Two new terms describe how to stay visible in this world: **AEO** (Answer Engine Optimization) and **GEO** (Generative Engine Optimization). This post explains what each one means, how they differ from SEO, how they actually work, and what they mean for businesses.

## SEO: Ranking Pages

**Search Engine Optimization (SEO)** is the practice of helping your pages rank in traditional search results.

A search engine works in three stages:

- **Crawling:** bots visit your pages and follow links to discover new ones.
- **Indexing:** the engine stores and understands what each page is about.
- **Ranking:** when someone searches, the engine orders the pages it thinks best answer the query, using signals such as relevance, links from other sites, content quality and page experience.

The result is a list of links. Success means appearing high on that list and getting the click. SEO is measured in rankings, impressions, clicks and website traffic.

## AEO: Becoming the Answer

**Answer Engine Optimization (AEO)** is the practice of making your content the direct answer to a question.

Answer engines do not just list pages. They pull out a specific passage and show it, or read it aloud, as the answer. Examples include:

- **Featured snippets:** the answer box at the top of Google results.
- **People Also Ask:** the expandable list of related questions and short answers.
- **Voice assistants:** Siri, Alexa and Google Assistant, which usually read out a single answer.
- **Knowledge panels:** the information box about a person, business or place.

### How AEO Works

Answer engines look for content that answers a question clearly and can be lifted out on its own. In practice, that means:

- **A question as a heading**, such as "How long does a passport renewal take?", followed by a direct answer in the first sentence or two.
- **Concise answers** of around 40 to 60 words, with more detail afterwards.
- **Well-structured formats**, such as numbered steps for processes, bullet lists for options and short definitions for terms.
- **Structured data** (schema markup), which describes your content to machines in a standard format. It labels things such as your business, a person, a product, an article or a set of frequently asked questions. Which rich results Google displays changes over time, but structured data still helps engines understand what a page contains.

AEO is measured by whether you hold featured snippets, appear in People Also Ask, and are the answer for the questions your customers ask.

## GEO: Being Cited by AI

**Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)** is the practice of getting your brand, content and expertise included in answers written by AI.

Generative engines include ChatGPT search, Perplexity, Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, Microsoft Copilot, Gemini and Claude. Instead of showing a list of links or one extracted passage, they write a new answer, combining information from many sources and sometimes citing them.

The term comes from a 2023 research paper, "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization", by researchers from Princeton University, IIT Delhi and other institutions. They found that some changes to content, such as adding citations to sources, quotations from experts and relevant statistics, increased how visible that content was in AI-generated answers by up to around 40% in their tests.

### How Generative Engines Choose Sources

Most AI search tools follow a process often called retrieval-augmented generation:

- **Understanding the question:** the model works out what the user is really asking, often splitting it into several smaller searches.
- **Retrieving sources:** it searches the web or its own index for relevant pages.
- **Selecting passages:** it picks the passages that best support an answer.
- **Writing the answer:** it combines those passages into a new response, sometimes with links to the sources it used.

This means GEO depends on two things. Your content has to be **retrievable**, which means it is indexed and AI crawlers are allowed to read it. It also has to be **worth quoting**: clear, specific, factual and trustworthy.

AI models also form an impression of your brand from what the rest of the web says about it: reviews, news articles, forums, comparison pages and directories. A business that is frequently and consistently described in the same way across many sites is more likely to be mentioned in an AI answer.

## How They Differ

The three overlap, but each has a different goal:

- **SEO** aims to rank a page in a list of results. Success is a click to your website.
- **AEO** aims to be the single answer to a specific question. Success is being shown or read out as that answer, often without a click.
- **GEO** aims to be part of an AI-written answer. Success is being mentioned, recommended or cited, even when the user never visits your site.

They also differ in what they reward:

- **SEO** rewards relevant pages, strong links and good technical health.
- **AEO** rewards clear, well-structured answers to specific questions.
- **GEO** rewards content that is factual, specific and easy to quote, plus a brand that the rest of the web talks about.

The most important point: **AEO and GEO build on SEO; they do not replace it.** AI search tools and answer engines still rely on crawling and indexing the web. If search engines cannot find, crawl or trust your pages, neither answer engines nor AI tools will use them.

## What This Means for Businesses

### Fewer Clicks for Simple Questions

When the answer appears directly on the results page or in an AI response, many users never click through. SparkToro's 2024 study of Google searches found that more than half of searches in the US and the EU ended without a click to any website.

For businesses that relied on blog traffic from simple informational questions, this means fewer visitors, even when their content is used in the answer.

### Better Visitors When They Do Click

The visitors who do arrive from AI answers have often already compared their options. They click because an AI tool recommended you for their specific need, which can make them much more likely to get in touch or buy.

### Being Recommended Becomes the New Ranking

People increasingly ask AI tools questions like "What is the best project management tool for a small agency?" or "Which dentist near me is open on Sundays?". If your business is not part of the answer, you are not part of the decision. Gartner predicted in 2024 that traditional search engine volume would drop by 25% by 2026 as people move to AI chatbots and virtual agents.

### Brand Matters More Than Ever

Because AI answers often name brands without linking to them, being known and described consistently across the web becomes a competitive advantage. Reviews, press mentions, partnerships and genuine expertise all feed into how AI tools describe you.

### Measurement Gets Harder

Traditional SEO has clear numbers: rankings and clicks. AI visibility is harder to track, because answers change from one user and conversation to the next. Businesses need new ways to measure, which are covered below.

## A Practical Checklist

You do not need three separate strategies. One set of good habits covers all three.

### Get the Foundations Right (SEO)

- Make sure your site is fast, works well on mobile and is easy for search engines to crawl.
- Submit a sitemap and check for indexing problems in Google Search Console.
- Give every page a clear, unique title and description.

### Answer Questions Directly (AEO)

- Find the real questions your customers ask, from sales calls, support tickets and tools such as Google's People Also Ask.
- Use those questions as headings, and answer each one in the first sentence or two below it.
- Add structured data for your business, articles, products and people.

### Be Worth Quoting (GEO)

- Include specific facts, numbers, examples and clear definitions instead of vague claims.
- Cite your sources, and add quotes from real experts where they help.
- Keep important pages up to date, and show when they were last updated.
- Check that your `robots.txt` does not block the AI search crawlers you want to be visible in, such as OAI-SearchBot for ChatGPT search and PerplexityBot for Perplexity.
- Build your reputation off your own site: reviews, directory listings, guest articles and genuine participation in communities where your customers ask questions.

### Measure What You Can

- Track featured snippets and People Also Ask appearances for your main questions.
- Look for visits from AI tools in your analytics. Referral sources such as `chatgpt.com` and `perplexity.ai` show traffic from AI answers.
- Regularly ask AI tools the questions your customers would ask, and note whether and how your business appears.
- Watch branded search volume. More people searching for your name is often a sign that AI tools are mentioning you.

## A Real Example: This Website

When I set up SEO for this portfolio, the same work covered all three:

- **SEO:** a sitemap, a `robots.txt` file, canonical URLs and a clear title and description for every page.
- **AEO:** articles structured around the questions developers actually search for, with direct answers under clear headings.
- **GEO:** structured data that describes who I am, what I do, where I work and which profiles belong to me, so search engines and AI tools can connect my name, my work and my writing.

None of these are tricks. They make it easier for every kind of engine, traditional or generative, to understand the site and trust it.

## Conclusion

SEO gets your pages ranked. AEO gets your content chosen as the answer. GEO gets your brand included when AI writes the answer. They are not competing strategies but layers of the same goal: being the most useful, clear and trustworthy source on your topic.

For businesses, the shift is real. There will be fewer clicks for simple questions, but more weight on reputation and more valuable visitors from AI recommendations. The businesses that do well will be the ones that keep the SEO basics strong, answer their customers' questions directly and build a brand that the rest of the web, and every AI tool reading it, can vouch for.
